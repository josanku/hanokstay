# Hanok Regions of Korea — Reference Sheet (EN)

> Internal reference for content, AI curator training, and host onboarding. Each entry is structured so it can be reused as listing-page copy, KTO submission, or press material.

---

## 1. Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌한옥마을)

- **Location**: Jongno-gu, Seoul. Between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung Palaces.
- **Established**: 14th century as royal scholars' quarter; rebuilt as modern hanok district 1920s–30s.
- **Scale**: ~900 hanoks remain. Designated Hanok Preservation Area by Seoul Metropolitan Government.
- **Character**: Tight urban grid, narrow stone-walled lanes (골목), tile-roofed skyline visible from multiple viewpoints (Bukchon 8 Views — 북촌 8경).
- **Why stay here**: Walk to two UNESCO palaces in 10 minutes. Insadong, Samcheong-dong, and Anguk Station within 5 minutes. Best for: first-time visitors, palace itineraries, history-focused stays.
- **Don't expect**: Quiet during daytime. Bukchon receives 10,000+ daytime visitors. Best experienced at dawn and after 7 PM.
- **Closest subway**: Anguk Station (Line 3).

## 2. Seochon (서촌)

- **Location**: Jongno-gu, Seoul. West of Gyeongbokgung Palace.
- **Established**: Same era as Bukchon, but historically the artists' and middle-class quarter, not the aristocrats'.
- **Character**: Quieter, more residential, more lived-in than Bukchon. Bookshops, small galleries, neighborhood restaurants inside 1920s hanok shells. The poet Yi Sang and painter Park Soo-keun lived here.
- **Why stay here**: For travelers who want hanok atmosphere without Bukchon's tourist density. Better for repeat visitors to Seoul.
- **Closest subway**: Gyeongbokgung Station (Line 3).

## 3. Ikseon-dong (익선동)

- **Location**: Jongno-gu, Seoul. South of Jongno 3-ga subway.
- **Established**: Korea's first planned urban hanok development, 1920s.
- **Character**: The "hanok hipster" alley. ~150 hanoks tightly packed in a small block, now hosting cafés, vintage shops, and modern restaurants inside hanok shells. The most Instagram-popular hanok cluster in Seoul.
- **Why stay here**: Nightlife adjacent, modern Seoul young culture, walkable to Jongmyo Shrine.
- **Closest subway**: Jongno 3-ga Station (Lines 1, 3, 5).

## 4. Jeonju Hanok Village (전주 한옥마을)

- **Location**: Wansan-gu, Jeonju, North Jeolla Province. 2 hr from Seoul by KTX.
- **Scale**: Largest hanok cluster in Korea — 800+ hanoks, almost all residential or commercial (vs museum/preserved).
- **Character**: Lived-in. Locals actually live in many of these hanoks. The neighborhood is a UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy — Jeonju is the birthplace of bibimbap, mukeunji (aged kimchi), and Korean kongnamul gukbap. Hanbok rental is universal here.
- **Adjacent sites**: Gyeonggijeon Shrine (Joseon Dynasty founder's portrait), Jeondong Catholic Cathedral, Pungnammun Gate.
- **Why stay here**: The "village hanok experience" — wandering, eating, slow days. Easy from Seoul. Best for: first-time outside-Seoul travelers, families, food-focused stays.

## 5. Gyeongju (경주)

- **Location**: North Gyeongsang Province. Capital of the Silla Dynasty (57 BC – 935 AD).
- **Character**: An entire city built around royal burial mounds. UNESCO World Heritage sites scattered through the city. Hanok stays here range from modern hanok-style guesthouses in central Gyeongju to genuinely old aristocratic hanoks in Yangdong Folk Village (양동마을, UNESCO).
- **Yangdong Folk Village**: 500-year-old aristocratic clan village, ~150 hanoks preserved as the families' descendants still live there. One of the deepest hanok experiences possible.
- **Why stay here**: Korea's deepest history. Best for: history-focused travelers, slow itineraries.

## 6. Andong (안동) — Hahoe Village (하회마을)

- **Location**: North Gyeongsang Province, ~3 hr from Seoul.
- **UNESCO**: Hahoe Village is UNESCO World Heritage (2010).
- **Character**: A hanok village inside a bend in the Nakdong River. Founded 600 years ago as a single-clan village of the Pungsan Ryu lineage. Still inhabited by Ryu descendants. Confucian scholar tradition, the famous Hahoe mask dance (UNESCO Intangible Heritage), Andong soju.
- **Why stay here**: Most "lived" UNESCO hanok experience. Best for: Confucian-history, traditional-craft, intangible-heritage travelers.

## 7. Jeju (제주)

- **Location**: Volcanic island, 1 hr flight from Seoul.
- **Character**: Jeju hanok is a different architecture from mainland hanok. Walls of black volcanic basalt, dry-stacked. Roofs thatched and roped against typhoons in a distinctive grid (jeongnang). Doorways are low. Inside, dirt floors traditionally, with raised wooden sleeping platforms.
- **Why stay here**: Architectural distinctness — Jeju hanok is its own thing. Combine with hiking, sea, oreum volcanic cones.

## 8. Gangneung (강릉)

- **Location**: East Coast, Gangwon Province. 2 hr from Seoul by KTX.
- **Notable**: Ojukheon (오죽헌) — a 500-year-old hanok where Korea's greatest Confucian philosopher Yi I (Yulgok) was born. Home of his mother Shin Saimdang, who is on the Korean 50,000-won note.
- **Character**: Pine forests, the East Sea, anchovy-broth breakfasts. Smaller hanok cluster than Jeonju but more intimate. Adjacent to Korea's surfing coast (Sokcho).
- **Why stay here**: Sea + tradition combination. Best for: couples, retreat-style stays.

- **Hadong / Jirisan area** — mountain hanok, tea culture.
- **Tongyeong / Hansando** — southern coast, sea views.
- **Boseong** — green tea fields + hanok stays.
